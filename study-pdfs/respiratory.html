Part 1 — Anatomy Day 1

Lung Anatomy

  • Right lung: 3 lobes (upper, middle, lower), 10 segments — wider & shorter.
  • Left lung: 2 lobes (upper, lower), 8–9 segments — longer & narrower (cardiac notch).
  • Right main bronchus is more vertical → foreign body lodges here.
  • Carina: at T4 / angle of Louis — most sensitive cough reflex point.
  • Hilum contents (from posterior → anterior): bronchus, pulmonary artery (above), veins (below/front).

Pleura

  • Visceral pleura: no pain fibers (autonomic only).
  • Parietal pleura: pain fibers — intercostal & phrenic nerves.
  • Costodiaphragmatic recess: most dependent — fluid collects here first.

Diaphragm — Openings

Mnemonic: I 8 (ate) 10 eggs At 12.

Muscles of Breathing

  • Inspiration: Diaphragm (chief), External intercostals; accessory = SCM, scalenes.
  • Expiration: Passive at rest. Forced expiration = Internal intercostals + abdominals.

Dead Space

  • Anatomical: conducting airways ≈ 150 mL (measured by Fowler’s method).
  • Alveolar: ventilated but not perfused (V/Q = ∞).
  • Physiological: anatomical + alveolar (measured by Bohr’s method).

V/Q Ratio

  • Normal V/Q = 0.8.
  • Apex: V/Q = 3.3 — over-ventilated, under-perfused. TB loves the apex.
  • Base: V/Q = 0.6 — under-ventilated, over-perfused.
  • V/Q = 0 → shunt (perfused, not ventilated).
  • V/Q = ∞ → dead space (ventilated, not perfused).

Oxygen Transport

  • 98.5% on Hb · 1.5% dissolved in plasma.
  • 1 g Hb carries 1.34 mL O₂ (Hüfner’s constant).
  • Hb 15 g/dL → O₂ capacity ≈ 20.1 mL/dL.

Oxygen Dissociation Curve

Right shift (↑ O₂ release)Left shift (↑ Hb affinity)
↑ CO₂ (Bohr) · ↑ H⁺ · ↑ Temp · ↑ 2,3-DPG · Exercise ↓ CO₂ · ↓ H⁺ · ↓ Temp · ↓ 2,3-DPG · HbF · CO poisoning · MetHb

CO₂ Transport

  • 70% as bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻) — dominant.
  • 23% as carbamino-Hb · 7% dissolved.
  • Haldane effect: oxygenation promotes CO₂ release.

Control of Breathing

  • Central chemoreceptors (medulla): respond to CO₂/H⁺ — main driver.
  • Peripheral (carotid, aortic bodies): respond to PO₂ < 60 mmHg, ↑ CO₂, ↓ pH.
  • In COPD CO₂-retainers, drive shifts to hypoxia → never give high-flow O₂.

Surfactant

  • Made by Type II pneumocytes; main lipid = DPPC.
  • ↓ surface tension · prevents alveolar collapse · stabilizes small alveoli.
  • Deficiency → IRDS in preterm babies.
  • Laplace: P = 2T / r. Without surfactant, small alveoli collapse into large ones.

Part 3 — Pathology Day 4–5

Obstructive Lung Diseases

Emphysema

  • Permanent alveolar enlargement + wall destruction (no fibrosis).
  • Centriacinar: upper lobe, smokers (most common).
  • Panacinar: lower lobe, α1-antitrypsin deficiency (PiZZ — liver cirrhosis + emphysema).
  • Paraseptal: subpleural → spontaneous pneumothorax in tall young males.
  • Pathogenesis: smoking → neutrophil/macrophage elastase ↑ → alveolar destruction.

Chronic Bronchitis

  • Clinical: productive cough ≥ 3 mo/yr for ≥ 2 consecutive years.
  • Reid index > 0.5 (mucous gland hypertrophy).
  • "Blue bloater" — cyanosis, edema, hypercapnia, polycythemia.

Asthma

  • Reversible airflow obstruction.
  • Sputum: Curschmann spirals + Charcot-Leyden crystals.
  • Goblet cell hyperplasia · subepithelial fibrosis · smooth muscle hypertrophy · eosinophils.
  • Status asthmaticus → severe attack unresponsive to bronchodilators.

Bronchiectasis

  • Permanent abnormal bronchial dilatation.
  • Causes: CF (West), TB (India), post-measles/pertussis, Kartagener (situs inversus + sinusitis + bronchiectasis).
  • Sputum: 3 layers — frothy, mucopurulent, purulent.

Part 4 — Medicine Day 6–8 · Highest FMGE Yield

Asthma Management (GINA)

Step 1: SABA (salbutamol) PRN
Step 2: Low-dose ICS + SABA PRN
Step 3: Low ICS + LABA  OR  Medium ICS
Step 4: Medium/high ICS + LABA
Step 5: + tiotropium / biologics (omalizumab, mepolizumab)

Acute Severe Asthma

  • O₂ to SpO₂ ≥ 94%.
  • Nebulized SABA + ipratropium.
  • IV/oral prednisolone.
  • IV MgSO₄ (bronchodilator).
DANGER SIGN: Normal or rising PaCO₂ in acute asthma = exhaustion / impending respiratory arrest.

COPD

Spirometry: FEV1/FVC < 0.7 post-bronchodilator.

GOLD Staging (FEV1 % predicted)

  • GOLD 1: ≥ 80% (mild) · GOLD 2: 50–79% · GOLD 3: 30–49% · GOLD 4: < 30%.

Stable Treatment (GOLD groups)

A (low sx, low risk):  SABA / SAMA PRN
B (high sx, low risk): LABA or LAMA
E (high exacerbation): LABA + LAMA
                       + ICS if eosinophils > 300

Acute Exacerbation

  • Controlled O₂ (target 88–92%).
  • Bronchodilators (SABA + SAMA) + Prednisolone 40 mg × 5 d.
  • Antibiotics if purulent sputum (amoxicillin / doxy / azithromycin).
  • NIV (BiPAP) if pH < 7.35 with ↑ PaCO₂.
LTOT: PaO₂ ≤ 55 mmHg or SpO₂ ≤ 88% → ≥ 15 hrs/day.

Pneumonia

  • Most common organism in CAP: Streptococcus pneumoniae.
  • Atypicals: Mycoplasma, Legionella, Chlamydia — treat with macrolide / doxy / FQ.

CURB-65 (hospitalization)

  • C: Confusion · U: Urea > 7 mmol/L · R: RR ≥ 30 · B: SBP < 90 or DBP ≤ 60 · 65: Age ≥ 65
  • 0–1 outpatient · 2 hospital · ≥3 consider ICU.

Legionella

  • Source: contaminated water (AC, cooling towers).
  • Hyponatremia + ↑ LFT + diarrhea + altered sensorium.
  • Diagnose: urinary antigen. Treat: azithromycin / FQ (not amoxicillin).

Aspiration pneumonia: right lower lobe (upright) or right upper lobe posterior segment (supine).

Pulmonary Embolism

  • Virchow’s triad: stasis + hypercoagulability + endothelial injury.
  • Sudden dyspnea + pleuritic chest pain + hemoptysis + DVT signs.
  • ECG: S1Q3T3 (classic); sinus tachy (most common).
  • Investigation of choice: CT pulmonary angiography (CTPA).
  • D-dimer: high sensitivity, low specificity — a negative result rules out PE in low-probability patients.
  • Echo: RV strain, McConnell sign.

Treatment

  • Stable: anticoagulation (LMWH → warfarin / DOAC).
  • Massive PE + shock: thrombolysis (alteplase).
  • Surgical embolectomy if thrombolysis contraindicated.

ARDS — Berlin Definition

  • Onset ≤ 1 week of trigger.
  • Bilateral infiltrates (not from effusion / collapse).
  • P/F ratio: Mild 200–300 · Moderate 100–200 · Severe < 100.

Management

  • Lung-protective ventilation: TV 6 mL/kg IBW.
  • PEEP to maintain oxygenation.
  • Prone position if P/F < 150.
  • No routine steroids (except late fibroproliferative phase).

Part 5 — Pharmacology Day 9

Bronchodilators — β2 Agonists

DrugDurationUse
SalbutamolShort 4–6 hAcute relief (SABA)
FormoterolLong 12 hFastest-onset LABA
SalmeterolLong 12 hMaintenance — slow onset
IndacaterolUltra-long 24 hOnce-daily COPD

Anticholinergics (M3 blockers)

  • Ipratropium (SAMA): 3–4×/day, more COPD.
  • Tiotropium (LAMA): once daily, ↓ COPD exacerbations.

Methylxanthines

  • Theophylline — narrow therapeutic index (toxic > 20 µg/mL).
  • SE: arrhythmia, seizures, GI upset.
  • Erythromycin, cimetidine ↑ levels (enzyme inhibition).

Inhaled Corticosteroids (ICS)

  • Beclomethasone · budesonide · fluticasone · ciclesonide.
  • SE: oral candidiasis (rinse mouth), dysphonia, HPA suppression (high-dose).
  • Ciclesonide = prodrug → activated in lung → minimal systemic SE.

Leukotriene Antagonists

  • Montelukast, zafirlukast — CysLT1 blockers.
  • Aspirin-exacerbated asthma · exercise-induced · mild persistent.
  • Aspirin-sensitive asthma: arachidonic acid shunted to leukotrienes — avoid NSAIDs.

Mucolytics & Antitussives

  • N-acetylcysteine: breaks disulfide bonds; also paracetamol antidote.
  • Bromhexine · Ambroxol (its active metabolite).
  • Guaifenesin — expectorant.
  • Dextromethorphan — central NMDA antagonist (antitussive).
  • Codeine — opioid antitussive.

Quick-Fire Revision Day 10

Read this list aloud the night before any test

  • Foreign body → right main bronchus (more vertical).
  • Surfactant → Type II pneumocyte; main lipid DPPC.
  • RV / FRC / TLC → cannot be measured by spirometry.
  • FEV1/FVC < 0.7 → obstructive; ratio normal/high → restrictive.
  • Right shift ODC → ↑ CO₂, ↑ H⁺, ↑ T°, ↑ 2,3-DPG.
  • HbF and CO → left shift.
  • SCLC → SIADH, ACTH, Lambert-Eaton.
  • Squamous cell → PTHrP → hypercalcemia.
  • Legionella → urinary antigen + azithromycin.
  • Tension pneumothorax → needle 2nd ICS MCL — before CXR.
  • Isoniazid → peripheral neuropathy → give pyridoxine (B6).
  • Rifampicin → enzyme inducer + orange urine.
  • Ethambutol → optic neuritis (red-green).
  • ARDS → TV 6 mL/kg + prone if P/F < 150.
  • V/Q at apex = 3.3 (TB zone); base = 0.6.
  • Reid index > 0.5 → chronic bronchitis.
  • α1-AT deficiency → panacinar emphysema (lower lobe).
  • Light’s criteria: protein ratio > 0.5 or LDH ratio > 0.6 → exudate.
  • NTEP new TB regimen: 2 HRZE / 4 HR.
  • Massive PE + shock → thrombolysis (alteplase).
